## 1. What RootCause is

RootCause lets residents report physical hazards on public property and lets the Department of Public Works rank, schedule and verify the work. Browsing the map and reports needs no account. Reporting, voting, commenting, following and verifying need one. Using the app means you accept these terms.

## 2. Emergencies

RootCause is not an emergency service. **If anyone is in danger right now, call 911.** Choosing "Emergency" on a report pages the on-call supervisor and places the report at the top of the queue; it does not replace 911. Emergency reports are limited to one per account per day, and misuse can lead to a block (section 7).

## 3. Accounts

- One account per person, through Sign in with Apple, Google, or an email code. You are responsible for what is done with your account.
- Staff accounts are granted by the public-works director and are subject to the city's own policies in addition to these terms.
- You can delete your account at any time (Me → Delete my data). What that removes and what it keeps is in the privacy notice.
- `[legal: minimum age — 13 or 18]`

## 4. What you may report

Report hazards on the public right-of-way and city assets: sidewalks, street trees and vegetation, roadways, storm drains, street lighting. Do not report:

- private property, people, vehicles or businesses (private-property concerns go to code enforcement, not to this app);
- anything that is not a physical hazard (noise, parking, animals);
- faces, licence plates or house interiors — keep them out of frame; version 1 does not blur them.

Reports, votes and comments must be truthful. The two questions on the form — how dangerous it is right now and whether anyone has been hurt — change the priority score and, for injuries, notify the city's risk management. Answer them honestly.

## 5. Your content and the licence you grant

You keep ownership of the photos and text you submit. By submitting them you grant the city a perpetual, worldwide, royalty-free licence to store, copy, resize, analyse (including with the vision assist described in the privacy notice), publish on the public map and web pages, include in exports and Open311 feeds, and use for hazard management, claims defence and audits. `[legal: confirm the licence scope — D12]`

Everything you submit is a public record and may be released under the New Jersey Open Public Records Act or other law, including your photos. `[legal: D12 — confirm wording before launch]`

You confirm that you took the photo or have the right to submit it, and that it was taken from a place you were allowed to be.

## 6. Identity on a report

You choose, per report, to appear as Named, Initials, or Anonymous. Anonymous reports count toward the priority score but cannot receive status updates, because the server keeps no link between the report and your account. Choosing Anonymous does not exempt the report itself from being a public record.

## 7. Votes, comments, and what gets you blocked

- One vote per account per report. Votes are weighted (new accounts and accounts without a home watch area count less), per-capita normalised, saturating and capped, so a block cannot shout its way to the top; the method is published on the "Why this score" screen and the equity page.
- Comments are public records and are filtered for links and abuse. Staff can hide a comment or photo; any user can flag one; you can block another user, which hides their content from you.
- Coordinated voting, multiple accounts, false emergencies, false injury answers, harassment, and reporting people rather than hazards can lead to the removal of content, the down-weighting of your votes, or a block. Blocks are logged. To appeal, write to `[contact email]`.

## 8. The priority score and the city's work

The score is a published formula (five weighted terms, shown for every report), not a promise. A rank, an SLA band or a "next steps" line describes the city's target, not a guarantee that work will happen by a date. The city decides what to schedule. Reports marked "Not city-owned" name who owns the asset where known. `[legal: no-reliance wording]`

## 9. AI assist

When vision assist is on, a model proposes a category and sub-type from your photo. It is a proposal: you correct it, an inspector confirms it, and the app never shows a measurement or an "emergency" from the model alone. The assist can be off at any time and the report flow is the same without it.

## 10. Alerts and SMS terms

**Push.** Status changes on your own and followed reports; in a later release, storm advisories for your watch areas. At most two predictive alerts in any 7 days; your quiet hours are respected; only an emergency alert approved by the public-works director overrides both.
